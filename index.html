🚦 TrafficMind: Navi Mumbai AI Signal Optimizer

Can PSO beat fixed-time signals in Navi Mumbai?

A real microscopic simulation (IDM car-following, seeded Poisson arrivals) and a real Particle Swarm Optimizer whose fitness is computed by that simulator. Tested against simulated equivalents of major Navi Mumbai junctions.

Conventional fixed-time

Green durations are set in advance (default 30 s + 30 s, 3 s amber, 2 s all-red) and never change during a run. Simple and predictable, but blind to which approach actually has the queue.

PSO-optimized timing

Each particle is a candidate plan x=[g₁,g₂]. The swarm searches for green splits that minimize a delay-and-queue objective measured by simulation. An optional rolling-horizon mode re-optimizes from observed arrivals at cycle boundaries.

Why fixed-time plans struggle

When demand shifts (e.g. heavy north–south flow), an equal split wastes green on the lighter approach while the busy one builds a queue that cannot discharge in its green. PSO can re-balance the split. It is not guaranteed to win: a plan tuned to one demand pattern can do worse when demand changes mid-run (the "Sudden surge" scenario can show this).

PSO in simple words

Particle Swarm Optimization is a population-based algorithm inspired by the collective movement of birds and fish. Each particle represents a possible timing plan; particles explore and gradually move toward plans that reduce delay. It does not guarantee a global optimum.

Explain PSO visually

  1. Generate particles — random green plans [g₁,g₂].
  2. Simulate each plan and compute fitness J = α·D/D_ref + β·Q/Q_ref (D: mean delay, Q: time-avg queue, both normalized by the fixed-time baseline; α=1, β=0.3). Unserved vehicles still count their delay.
  3. Personal best pᵢ per particle.
  4. Global best g for the swarm.
  5. Update: vᵢ←w·vᵢ+c₁r₁(pᵢ−xᵢ)+c₂r₂(g−xᵢ), xᵢ←xᵢ+vᵢ (w 0.9→0.4, c₁=c₂=1.5).
  6. Repair to 7–60 s greens and a 110 s green budget, round to seconds.
  7. Repeat, then deploy the best feasible plan at a cycle boundary. No guarantee of a global optimum.

Research foundation

Paper 1 — Abdul Kareem & Mejbel, Traffic Light Controller Module Based on PSO, American Journal of Artificial Intelligence 2018, 2(1):7–15

  • PSO selects which set of adjacent streets receives green; the fitness is a linear priority score |PC+PE+PSW−PP| (crowded, emergency vehicle, roadwork, pedestrian), fed by a separate camera-based monitor module.
  • Reported "flow ratio" improvements of roughly 85–96% over several multi-light layouts; PSO needed about 80–90 iterations. The paper notes PSO must finish within the cycle time to be useful.
  • Limitations (our reading): no vehicle-level traffic simulation, so those percentages are not comparable to delay measured here.

Paper 2 — Diaf, Tolba & Nait Sidi Moh, Traffic Urban Control Using an Intelligent PSO Algorithm Based on Integrated Approach, Algerian Journal of Signals and Systems 2020, 5(1)

  • Each particle is a signal cycle program whose dimensions are phase durations; fitness uses a macroscopic LWR / continuous Petri-net model with Rakha vehicle dynamics and Poisson arrivals.
  • Reported tuning: swarm sizes 20–200, c₁=c₂=2.05, convergence around 200+ iterations, cycle times of about 71–74 s for a single intersection.
  • Limitations: macroscopic model, one tested intersection, no comparison to a fixed-time baseline in the portions we read.

Taken from the papers vs. our own choices

  • From the literature: particle = timing plan, phase durations as variables, standard velocity/position update, Poisson arrivals.
  • Our design: microscopic IDM simulator, delay+queue objective normalized by a baseline, held-out test seeds, repair step, rolling-horizon extension, fixed-time baseline.

Differences from real traffic management

Single lane per approach, straight movements only, no pedestrians, no sensor noise, perfect compliance, two phases. Rolling-horizon re-optimization here starts each internal simulation from an empty network using arrival rates estimated from past counts, and applies the result one cycle later — noisy estimates and compute time limit it. Future work: turning movements, pedestrian phases, emergency priority, sensor uncertainty, real data. Educational simulation only; not for deployment.
